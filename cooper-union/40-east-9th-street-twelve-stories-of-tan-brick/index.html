Every line traces one tower through a century of the same block — 1850s row houses, Agnes de Mille choreographing Rodeo across the street, Joseph Papp founding the Public Theater from its upper floors, and H.I. Feldman's 1959 tan‑brick tower rising off a raised garden terrace. Sourced line by line below.

02

“a twelve-story shaft rising off a raised garden line, / H.I. Feldman drew it, set it back from the curb's face”

40 East Ninth Street

"40 East Ninth Street, a twelve-story building to rise in the center of the block from a raised garden terrace... the architect clad in a light-colored tan brick and set back from the street in the center of the block." Stern. (1995) New York 1960, p. 223.

03

“Feldman split the palette, made the massing condescend / to the Village context — red brick offset the higher mass, / but the center wears tan”

40 East Ninth Street

"Feldman used red brick on the low-rise buildings at each end of the block, suggesting an effort to respond to the Village context and offset the mass of the higher building, which the architect clad in a light-colored tan brick." Stern. (1995) New York 1960, p. 223.

04

“Joseph Papp lived inside it — nineteen seventy-three to ninety-one, / the Public Theater's founder in the tower Feldman spun”

40 East 9th Street

"Joseph Papp... 40 East 9th Street, Manhattan The dynamic founder and impresario of the New York Shakespeare Festival/Public Theater... he created The Public Theater... and lived here from 1973 to 1991." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 855.

05

“Thirteen stories some folks counted, long aluminum canopy”

40 East Ninth Street

"now replaced by the 13-story apartment building with the long, aluminum canopy." William H. Honan. (1959) Greenwich Village Guide, p. 66.

“Drop me to eighteen fifty-three, Perris drew the deep, / seven structures on this lot, dwellings first-class steep — / brick or stone, slate roof and coped, framed extensions grown, / skylights lighting the top floor only”

40 East 9th Street

"In the 1853 Perris Maps, on 40 East 9th Street, there is reference to 7 structures; Brick or Stone dwellings first class (slate or metal roof & coped) with Framed Extension and skylights lighting the top floor only." Perris. 1853 Perris Maps, p. 61.

40 East 9th Street

"In the 1867 Dripps Plan, on 40 East 9th Street, there is reference to 15 structures; Building, Building, Building..." Dripps. 1867 Dripps Plan, p. 7.

40 East 9th Street

"In the 1885 Robinsons Atlas, on 40 East 9th Street, there is reference to 16 structures; Brick building... Stone building." Robinson. 1885 Robinsons Atlas, p. 12.

40 East 9th Street

15

“September nineteen-fifty — the announcement drops the block, / Harry Landes' syndicate came to clear the diverse stock. / Forty buildings relieved of load, a landscaped platform planned... Construction held till December nineteen fifty-two, / sixty East Ninth first, two-sixteen families running through. / By October fifty-four the site for forty stood cleared bare — / a twelve-story to rise in the center from a raised garden square”

40 East Ninth Street

"In early September 1950 plans were announced for the redevelopment of an entire block... the new development would replace a diverse collection of forty buildings with a landscaped platform... The developers, a syndicate headed by Harry Landes... Construction did not begin until December 1952, when work commenced on 60 East Ninth Street, a six-story building housing 216 families... By October 1954... the site for 40 East Ninth Street, a twelve-story building to rise in the center of the block from a raised garden terrace, ready to be cleared." Stern. (1995) New York 1960, p. 223.
