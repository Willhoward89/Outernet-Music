Verse 1

01

“Nineteen fifty-five, red-and-buff brick set the grid, / H.I. Feldman drew the massing where the old hotel is hid”

Lafayette Apartments

"Built in the mid-1950s on the site of the old Hotel and Cafe Lafayette... H. I. Feldman was the architect for the present red-and-buff brick structures which, with twelve floors of apartments above the street-level commercial space, seemed mammoth when they were built, but have been dwarfed by more recent buildings." Norton & Patterson, (1984) Living It Up, p. 199.

02

“thirty East Ninth Street, garage under, gar ramp down”

Garage Under / Gar Ramp

"In the 1955 Manhattan Land Book, on 30 East 9th Street, there is written 'GARAGE UNDER, GAR RAMP' as well as reference to 8 Structures; 1 Story Brick or Stone or Concrete building with Store... 6 Story Brick or Stone or Concrete building with Elevators, Brick or Stone or Concrete building, Frame building." Bromley, 1955 Manhattan Land Book, p. 30.

03

“Low-apartment Lafayette, they filed it 'LA' in the deco file”

LA (Low-apartment Lafayette)

"LA, 30 East 9th Street, Low-apartment Lafayette." Vlack, (1974) Art Deco Architecture in New York, p. 165.

II

Verse 2

04

“Eighteen fifty-three, the Perris maps recorded first, / seventeen brick-and-stone first-class dwellings on the earth — / slate-or-metal coped, with framed extension to the back, / skylights lit the top floor only down the dwelling stack”

Hotel Martin

"In the 1853 Perris Maps, on 30 East 9th Street, there is reference to 17 structures; Brick or Stone dwellings first class (slate or metal roof & coped) with Brick or Stone Extension and Framed Extension and skylights lighting the top floor only..." Perris, 1853 Perris Maps, p. 61.

05

“Eighteen sixty-seven, Dripps just wrote it 'Building' plain, / seventeen the same, the row that held before it came”

Hotel Martin

"In the 1867 Dripps Plan, on 30 East 9th Street, there is reference to 17 structures; Building, Building, Building..." Dripps, 1867 Dripps Plan, p. 7.

06

“Then eighteen eighty-five the Sub-Rosa Club took hold, / Hotel Martin at the corner”

Sub-Rosa Club

"In the 1885 Robinsons Atlas, on 30 East 9th Street, there is written 'SUB-ROSA CLUB, HOTEL MARTIN' as well as reference to 17 Structures; Brick building..." Robinson, 1885 Robinsons Atlas, p. 12.

07

“J.B. Martin's kitchen bold”

Hotel Martin

"The Martinique traces its ancestry back to one of the great originals of New York restaurateurs, J. B. Martin, who made himself and his food famous in the little Hotel Martin at Eighth Street." Chappell, (1925) The Restaurants of New York, p. 86.

08

“a French pension where Caruso, Melba, Nordica would dine”

Hotel Lafayette

"A rendezvous for opera and stage celebrities, the French pension was a living memory of New York's bygone French Quarter... Enrico Caruso, Nordica and Melba were habitues here in the Gay Nineties." William H. Honan, (1959) Greenwich Village Guide, p. 64.

09

“Cafe Martin, Parisian air, the fine kitchen and the wine”

Hotel Martin

"The Cafe Martin, with its fine kitchen and well stocked cellar, had a Parisian atmosphere that New Yorkers found to their taste and it soon gained a vogue that it retains to the present day." Brown, (1924) Valentine's Manual 1924, p. 172.

10

“Nineteen-oh-two the Orteig bought it, Lafayette its name, / twenty-five-thousand hung for Paris, Lindbergh flew the claim”

Hotel Lafayette

"In 1902 it was bought by Raymond Orteig—who put up a $25.000 prize for the first transatlantic airplane flight, won by Col. Charles A. Lindbergh." William H. Honan, (1959) Greenwich Village Guide, p. 64.

11

“Marble tables, racks of newspaper, the high bohemian den”

Hotel Lafayette

"The Lafayette Hotel... also had a European-style café with marble tables and newspapers on racks... the Lafayette was the unrivaled meeting place of high bohemia during the twenties." Edmiston & Cirino, (1976) Literary New York, p. 61.

Lafayette Apartments

Hotel Lafayette

Hotel Lafayette

"IMAGE CAPTION: THE HOTEL LAFAYETTE, by John Sloan" Morris, (1951) Incredible New York, p. 305.
