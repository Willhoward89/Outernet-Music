29 East 4th StreetAmber Fly, East Fourth

Every line traces one house through nearly 200 years — built in 1832 for Joseph Brewster, bought by hardware merchant Seabury Tredwell in 1835, held by his family for 98 years until the last daughter’s death, reopened as the Old Merchant’s House Museum in 1936, landmarked in 1965, and restored through the 1970s and again in 2011 — still standing against the hotel development pressing in next door. Verse 1

01

“Flemish bond at the base, eighteen-thirty-two the year it rose”

Old Merchant's House

"The 3 1⁄2-story row house is in a transitional late Federal-Greek Revival style. The exterior is basically Federal in design, distinguished by its Flemish bond brickwork." Postal & Dolkart. (2009) Guide to New York City Landmarks, p. 63.

02

“Joseph Brewster set the Joseph Brewster residence in stone”

Joseph Brewster residence

"Old Merchants House of New York, Inc./formerly Seabury Tredwell residence/originally Joseph Brewster residence, 29 E. 4th St. bet. the Bowery and Lafayette St. 1832." AIA. (1988) AIA Edition 3, p. 153.

03

“Ionic columns in antis carry the Seabury Tredwell House out the dark”

Seabury Tredwell House

"off-center entrance with lonic columns in antis, rusticated surround, fanlight, and archivolt with keystone. Federal with Greek Revival elements." Gille. (1982) Encyclopedia of NY, p. 456.

04

“modillion cornice reading rhythm where the Tredwell House meets sky”

Tredwell House

"Brick, 3 1/2 stories, rectangular, gabled roof, 2 gabled dormers with end returns and round arched windows, modillion cornice." Gille. (1982) Encyclopedia of NY, p. 456.

05

“but the Old Merchant's House holds Greek Revival opulence within: the Ionic column screen dividing parlor front from rear”

Old Merchant's House

"The interior, on the other hand, contains stylish Greek forms, notably the Ionic column screen that separates the front and rear parlors on the main floor." Postal & Dolkart. (2009) Guide to New York City Landmarks, p. 63.

06

“Eight-panel mahogany doors, six-over-six in the paneled wall, simulated Siena marble, a rosette in the entrance hall”

Old Merchant's House

"with its simulated Siena marble walls and highly decorative plasterwork, such as a rosette in the entranceway and cornice with egg-and-dart moldings. The front and rear parlors are identical, with 14-foot ceilings, eight-panel mahogany doors, and six-over-six windows." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 133.

07

“a secret chamber camouflaged inside the Merchant's House wall's core”

Merchant's House

"Secret chambers, in the best mystery story tradition, were built in on both sides of the two parlors and are so cleverly camouflaged that even a careful observer would never know they existed." Dunshee. (1952) As You Pass By, p. 219.

Old Merchant's House

Joseph Brewster residence

Res. Seabury Tredwell

"Res. Seabury Tredwell, 29 East Fourth Street, New York City, 1832 (architect unknown). Open to the public as 'The Old Merchant's House.'" Andrews. (1969) Architecture in New York, p. 47.

Seabury Treadwell House

Seabury Tredwell residence

"Mr. Tredwell. Senior, seems to have been a domineering reactionary of the old Tory school and is said to have been the last man in New York to wear a queue." Dunshee. (1952) As You Pass By, p. 219.

Old Merchant House

"She made her appearance in 1840 in the luxuriously canopied four-poster mahogany bed in the second floor front bedroom and died ninety-three years later in the same bed, in the same room." Dunshee. (1952) As You Pass By, p. 219.

“Eighteen-fifty-three the Perris map called it first-class brick and stone, slate-and-metal roof, an extension throwing skylight on the throne”

Old Merchant's House

"In the 1853 Perris Maps, on 29 East 4th Street, there is reference to 1 Structure; Brick or Stone dwellings first class (slate or metal roof & coped) with Brick or Stone Extension and skylights lighting the top floor only." Perris. 1853 Perris Maps, p. 60.

16

“Eighteen-sixty-five the patriarch died, the back parlor turned to dine”

Merchant's House Museum

"Mrs. Tredwell redecorated in the 1860's... after Mr. Tredwell's death in 1865 the back parlor was converted into a formal dining room." Kahn. (2002) City Secrets New York City, p. 125.

Merchant's House Museum

"the rare rosewood square-case piano... manufactured in the 1840s by the New York firm of Nunns & Fischer... The piano has six and a half octaves and a cast-iron sounding board." Binkowski. (1999) Musical New York, p. 98.

Merchant's House Museum

"Mrs. Tredwell's mirror is lit by gas jets shaped to look like morning glories twining around the edge." Kahn. (2002) City Secrets New York City, p. 125.

Old Merchant House

"By 1909, only Gertrude was left... she had the shutters on the parlor floor closed and they were never again opened." Dunshee. (1952) As You Pass By, p. 219.

Old Merchant House

“the roof leaking rain on plaster ceilings, ready to let go”

Old Merchant's House

"the house was discovered to have serious structural problems, and rain from a leaking roof and outside walls was threatening the magnificent plaster ceilings." Delaney & Lockwood. (1984) Greenwich Village, p. 95.

24

“Nineteen seventy-two, Joseph Roberto came to relieve the strain, bearing walls disintegrating, delicate railings gone to rot and stain”

Seabury Treadwell House

"The delicate ironwork of its baluster and railings is decaying and disintegrating... Funds raised from a variety of public and private sources were used to conduct a much-needed structural renovation in 1972, supervised by New York University architect Joseph Roberto." Stern. (1995) New York 1960, p. 1092.

25

“restoration nineteen-seventy-nine — off-white woodwork, acanthus in the tread”

Old Merchant's House

"the mahogany stairways are partially carved with acanthus leaves in high relief, and the woodwork here, as elsewhere in the house, is painted off-white." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 133.

“Two thousand eleven waterproofed the shell against the ground, then two thousand fourteen — the eight-story hotel approved to rise around”

Old Merchant's House

"in 2011, an exterior restoration of the building, including waterproofing, was undertaken. In April 2014, the Landmarks Preservation Commission approved the proposed construction of an eight-story hotel adjacent to the Museum." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 133.
