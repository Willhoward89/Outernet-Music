6 Taras Shevchenko PlaceOnion Domes Over Hall Place

Every line traces one lot through 170 years — a coal yard on the 1853 Perris maps, wiped clean by 1867, filled with brick stables and lofts through 1916, reduced to one word — “Garage” — in the 1930 land book, then transformed in 1977 into Apollinaire Osadca’s polychrome, high‑domed St. George’s Ukrainian Catholic Church, the granite anchor of Little Ukraine, replacing the Greek Revival temple with onion domes that once stood around the corner on Hall Place. “Drop to eighteen-fifty-three, the Perris ink still wet, a Coal Yard on the lot, dark heaps and soot and debt. St. George's Ruthenian Greek Church

"In the 1853 Perris Maps, on 6 Taras Shevchenko Place, there is written 'Coal Yard' as well as reference to 6 Structures; a framed dwelling, a brick or stone dwelling second class (slate or metal roof not coped), a brick or stone dwelling second class (slate or metal roof not coped), a framed brick or stone store first class, a brick or stone dwelling second class (slate or metal roof not coped) with stores under, a brick or stone dwelling first class (slate or metal roof & coped)." Perris. 1853 Perris Maps, p. 49.

“St. George's Ruthenian Greek Church, the thing they had to take and put down... It replaced the real thing — now the real thing's a lot, a parking lot at twenty-four to twenty-eight”

St. George's Ruthenian Greek Church

"It replaced the real thing —the humbler Greek Revival style St. George's Ruthenian Greek Church, now a parking lot at Nos, 24-28." AIA. (1988) AIA Edition 3, p. 164.

16

“Greek Revival, stolid, stately temple stone, two marbleized Doric columns in antis, standing alone, onion domes at either side of the pediment, absurd and not unpleasant — Byzantine murals overheard”

St. George's Ukrainian Church

"St. George's Ukrainian Church is a stately, stolid temple with two marbleized Doric columns in antis, of the most popular type of the 1830s. It has added the extraordinary but not unpleasant embellishment of onion domes at either side of the pediment and decorated the interior with overwhelming Byzantine murals." Huxtable. (1964) Classic New York, p. 83.
