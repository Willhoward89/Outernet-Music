Every line traces one address where two buildings became one number — Orlando Potter’s ornate 1881 Astor Place Building, its cast-iron Neo-Grec base and terra-cotta masks, fused at the property line with William Gompert’s stark white-brick 1908 Sinclair Building next door. I

Verse 1

01

“Seven-eighty-two, the Astor Place Building stands on stone, eighteen-eighty-one, Starkweather laid the bone”

Astor Place Building

"(21) Astor Place Building. (One Astor Place). 750 Broadway. 1881 STARKWEATHER & GIBBS. The Astor Place Building was originally designed as a hotel and boarding house." Hennessy. (2020) Walking Broadway, p. 83.

02

“Orlando Potter's money, cast-iron at the floor, Neo-Grec in the base where the pilaster held the door”

One Astor Place

"The building was commissioned by prominent politician and developer Orlando Potter... The first floor is cast iron enriched by neo-Grec details." Hennessy. (2020) Walking Broadway, p. 83.

03

“James L. Jackson's ironworks, trademarks in the pier, raised letters through the paint, crisp lettering still here”

Astor Place Building (One Astor Place)

"Iron trademarks of James L. Jackson, Iron Works, N.Y., are found at the base of several of the large square iron-clad pliers on both sides of the building. The many years of accumulated paint do not obscure the crisp raised letters." Gayle & Gillon. (1974) Cast Iron Architecture, p. 159.

04

“Shift the material up — red brick, terra-cotta brown, every floor its own face, corbelled masks looking down”

Astor Place Building

"Above, the material shifts to red brick with brown terra-cotta trim. Each floor has its own personality... enlivened by an imaginative variety of heads, capitals, corbels, and decorative panels." Hennessy. (2020) Walking Broadway, p. 83.

05

“Six kinds of keystone heads, foliated panel skin, deep-set fenestration where the segmental light falls in”

Astor Place Building

"In the terra-cotta keystones one can make out at least six different kinds of faces and masks." Gayle & Gillon. (1974) Cast Iron Architecture, p. 159.

06

“Robust and chunky Victorian, sculptural in the seam, a hotel that never opened — clothing lofts, a broken dream”

One Astor Place

"This chunky, robust, Victorian red-brick fantasy is a total delight. Countless fine embellishments, like keystone masks and foliated panels, give it sculptural depth." Dunlap. (1990) On Broadway, p. 102.

07

“Twelve stories to the north, white brick against the gray, the Sinclair Building rises — nineteen-oh-eight, Gompert's day. Giant geometric brackets crown a cornice line so high”

Sinclair Building

"SINCLAIR BUILDING, No. 752-54 1908, by William H. Gompert. [12 stories] Giant, geometric cornice brackets dominate this white-brick facade. Site of Sinclair House hotel." Dunlap. (1990) On Broadway, p. 102.

Verse 2

08

“Drop it to the bottom — eighteen thirty-two the frame, east side of Broadway built up wholly, before the tower came”

One Astor Place

"In 1832 the east side of Broadway, from Astor Place to Eighth Street, was wholly built up." De Leeuw. (1910) Both Sides of Broadway, p. 21.

09

“Fifty-three the Perris map reads brick and stone and store, first-class dwellings, coped roofs, framed extensions on the floor”

Sinclair Ho.

"In the 1853 Perris Maps, on 752 Broadway, there is reference to 11 structures; Brick or Stone dwellings first class (slate or metal roof & coped) with Store under... Brick or Stone dwellings first class (slate or metal roof & coped) with Framed Extension." Perris. 1853 Perris Maps, p. 61.

10

“Then the Sinclair House rose, corner of the Eighth — famous for American cookery, Ashman keeping late. Artemus Ward lodged there editing his page”

Sinclair House

"The Sinclair House, at the corner of Eighth Street, was famous for its American cookery... Mr. Ashman of the Sinclair... Artemus Ward lived at the Sinclair during his stay in New York as editor of 'Vanity Fair.'" Brown. (1924) Valentine's Manual 1924, p. 157.

11

“the American plan, 'Sinclair, seven-fifty-four' — a landlord of old-school man”

Sinclair

"American Plan...Sinclair, 754 Broadway." Appleton. (1879) Appleton's Dictionary of New York, p. 108.

12

“Robinson's atlas, eighteen-eighty-five, five brick structures penned, 'Sinclair House' written plain before the century's end”

Sinclair House

"In the 1885 Robinson Atlas, on 752 Broadway, there is written 'Sinclair House' as well as reference to 5 Structures; Brick building, Brick building, Brick building, Brick building, Brick building." Robinson. 1885 Robinson Atlas, p. 12.

13

“The hotel dream from Potter's block never held the guests it sought — converted into manufacture... By eighteen-eighty-three it fell to loft and clothing trade”

Astor Place Building

"Potter and Starkweather also collaborated on the seven-story Astor Place Building (1881-83)... Originally intended as a mixed-use building combining hotel and mercantile facilities, it was instead occupied by clothing manufacturers." Stern. (1999) New York 1880, p. 418.

14

“The Sinclair Ho. of eighteen-ninety-seven, four structures in the plot”

Sinclair Ho.

"In the 1897 Bromleys Atlas, on 752 Broadway, there is written 'Sinclair Ho.' as well as reference to 4 Structures; 7 Story Brick building, Brick building, Brick building, Brick building." Bromley. 1897 Bromleys Atlas, p. 16.

15

“Steel construction, fireproof, the frame no longer wood — one number, two buildings, the seam where the old blood stood”

Astor Pl. Bldg.

"In the 1916 Atlas of the Borough of Manhattan, on 752 Broadway, there is written 'Astor Pl. Bldg., Sinclair Bldg.'... 12 Story Brick Building with Store and Elevators and Fire proof building (steel construction) and 1 Story Brick Extension." G W Bromley & Co. 1916 Atlas of the Borough of Manhattan, p. 34.

16

“Nineteen forty-two the Manhattan Savings Bank moves through the door... seven-fifty-four the corner... till fifty-one it leaves, Madison Avenue instead”

Manhattan Savings Bank

"Savings bank formed in 1942 by the merger of three banks... After the merger the new bank set up headquarters at 754 Broadway, which moved in 1951 to 385 Madison Avenue." Jackson. (2010) The Encyclopedia of New York, p. 793.
