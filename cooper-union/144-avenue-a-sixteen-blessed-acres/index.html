“the ground of Tompkins Square Park, sixteen blessed acres in these tight and dense streets”

Tompkins Square Park

"[E 38.) Tompkins Square Park/originally Tompkins Square, E. 7th to E. 10th Sts., Avenue A to Avenue B. 1834. Another London (Bloomsbury) park surrounded by high-density, low-rise housing. Sixteen blessed acres in these tight and dense streets." AIA. (2000) AIA Edition 4, p. 174.

02

“graded and landscaped between 1835 and 1850, a promise of open space and waterfront views”

Tompkins Square Park

"Originally swampland, Tompkins Square Park was graded and landscaped between 1835 and 1850, essentially to attract a wealthy population east with the promise of a 10.5-acre open space and waterfront views." Ferrara. (2009) A Guide to Gangsters, p. 137.

03

“pathways repaved, lawns replanted, the drainage system replaced”

“It reopened August 25th, 1992, a midnight curfew back in place, the concrete bandshell gone — that breeding ground for graffiti torn from the southern end and hauled away”

Tompkins Square Park

"Pathways were repaved, lawns replanted, the drainage system replaced, and new wrought-iron and wood benches installed. The removal of the concrete bandshell erected in the 1960s in the southern end of the park— which Parks Department officials saw as a breeding ground for homelessness, drugs, and graffiti... It was replaced by a portable stage. When the park reopened on August 25, 1992, a midnight curfew was put in place." Stern. (2006) New York 2000, p. 345.

09

“Originally swampland used for snipe hunting”

Tompkins Square

"In 1834 John Jacob Astor sold the 10.5-acre (4.2-hectare) swamp (used for snipe hunting) to New York City for $93,000." Jackson. (2010) The Encyclopedia of New York, p. 1323.

10

“graded between 1835 and 1850 to draw a wealthy population east. Then the Civil War ripped the city apart and the plan for an upscale community was put on hold”

“The Bread Riots of 1857, the Draft Riots of 1863, the laborer-led Stonecutter Riots of 1874”

“in 1878, redesigned and reopened”

Tompkins Square Park

"Originally swampland, Tompkins Square Park was graded and landscaped between 1835 and 1850... With the sprawling settlement of arriving European immigrants and the Civil War ripping the city apart, plans for an upscale community were put on hold. For a better part of its history, protests and riots were a fairly regular occurrence on these grounds, most notably the Irish-led Bread Riots of 1857, the Civil War Draft Riots of 1863 and the laborer-led Stonecutter Riots of 1874... In 1878, Tompkins Square Park was redesigned and reopened to the public." Ferrara. (2009) A Guide to Gangsters, p. 137.

11

“This was the very center of Kleindeutschland, Little Germany — home to more than 150,000 Germans, a free library, a hospital, a town hall. Avenue B was the German Broadway, Avenue A the street for beer gardens and oyster saloons”

Tompkins Square

"In 1904, Tompkins Square was in the very center of a neighborhood called Kleindeutschland or Little Germany. The neighborhood was home to more than 150,000 Germans and German-Americans; a German free library, hospital, town hall, and schools." Kahn. (2002) City Secrets New York City, p. 146. And: "Avenue B, occasionally called the German Broadway, was the main commercial artery. Avenue A was the street for beer gardens, oyster saloons, and groceries." Mahon. (2021) Pretty Evil New York, p. 45.

12

“June 15th, 1904 — the General Slocum burned to the waterline, at least 1,021 women and children drowned or burnt, and within a very short time Kleindeutschland was no more”

Tompkins Square Park

"by the time the Slocum pulled into North Brother Island, at least 1,021 women and children had been drowned, killed by the churning wheel of the steamship as they tried to escape the boat, or burnt to death. Almost every family in Kleindeutschland lost a family member to the disaster and within a very short time Kleindeutschland was no more." Kahn. (2002) City Secrets New York City, p. 146.

13

“By 1866 the Seventh Regiment took the square as a parade ground, its ranks outgrowing the armory before the city gave them Park Avenue and 66th”

Tompkins Square

"By the 1860s it had more than 1000 members and had outgrown its armory at Tompkins Square. The city provided a square block at Park Avenue and 66th Street." Jackson. (2010) The Encyclopedia of New York, p. 1172.

“January 13th, 1874, seven thousand gathered, mounted police charged and injured hundreds" →”

Tompkins Square

"As 7000 men and women gather in Tompkins Square on Jan 13, organized by the International Working Association led by Karl Marx (not present) they listen to speeches about the First International. Mounted police charge the crowd injuring hundreds." Patterson. (2007) Resistance, p. 238.

17

“August 6th, 1988, the police charged the crowds in phalanxes using billy clubs, black tape over their badge numbers, nearly fifty demonstrators injured”

Tompkins Square Park

"on August 6, a midnight demonstration organized to protest the curfew turned into six hours of violent confrontations between demonstrators and some 450 police officers, who charged at the crowds in phalanxes using billy clubs... The Tompkins Square Park Riots resulted in the injury of nearly fifty demonstrators... many officers had put black tape over their badge numbers and in some cases removed their badges entirely." Stern. (2006) New York 2000, p. 345.
