Verse 1

01

“The Hamilton, nineteen-fifty, H.I. Feldman signed the name”

The Hamilton (No. 757)

"8th STREET: W. THE HAMILTON, No. 757 1950, by H.I. Feldman. [6 stories] This drab modern structure wraps around a large court." Dunlap. (1990) On Broadway, p. 102.

02

“Six stories wrapping round a court, red brick against the Village grain”

Hamilton H. I. Feldman (The Hamilton)

"Feldman used red brick on the low-rise buildings at each end of the block, suggesting an effort to respond to the Village context." Stern. (1995) New York 1960, p. 223.

03

“Two-thirty-five feet on Eighth and Ninth, one-eighty-eight on Broadway's face”

Hamilton H. I. Feldman (The Hamilton)

"60 East Ninth Street, a six-story building housing 216 families, with 235 feet of frontage on both Eighth and Ninth streets and 188 feet on the west blockfront of Broadway." Stern. (1995) New York 1960, p. 223.

04

“Cast in nineteen-fifty-two, complete by fifty-four”

Hamilton H. I. Feldman (The Hamilton)

"Construction did not begin until December 1952, when work commenced on 60 East Ninth Street... By October 1954 it was complete." Stern. (1995) New York 1960, p. 223.

05

“where Lillian Russell's cozy rooms received Jim Brady at the door”

Lillian Russell (residence)

"The famous 'golden girl' of the Gay Nineties... lived in a cozy and luxurious apartment on this site at the turn of the century. Here she was often picked up by the flamboyant playboy Diamond Jim Brady in a Hansom cab." Plumb. (1989) The Streets Where They Lived, p. 150.

06

“her building gone, renumbered now, the modern slab”

Lillian Russell (residence)

"Russell's apartment building was replaced by this modern apartment building called the Hamilton (renumbered 60 East 9th Street)." Plumb. (1989) The Streets Where They Lived, p. 150.

Albion Hotel

American Hotel (Carter Building)

10

“Perris mapped it fifty-three, coped roofs and metal, dwellings grown”

1853 Perris Maps

"In the 1853 Perris Maps, on 60 East 9th Street, there is reference to 15 structures; Brick or Stone dwellings first class (slate or metal roof & coped)." Perris. 1853 Perris Maps, p. 61.

11

“By 'sixty-five John Daniell traded silks and lace and ribbon fine”

Dry Goods Daniels & Sons / John Daniell & Son / John Daniell & Sons, Haberdashers

"John Daniell was at 735 Broadway in 1865 and John Daniell & Sons is at 765 now, just opposite Wanamaker... they still pay special attention to silks, laces, embroidery and ribbons." Brown. (1921) Valentine's Manual 1921, p. 108.

13

Troy City Laundry

"'Daniell & Son, John Daniell & Son FANCY GOODS, TROY CITY LAUNDRY' as well as reference to 15 Structures." Robinson. 1885 Robinsons Atlas, p. 12.

“Then Schwarz in eighteen-seventy opened up his small Bazaar”

F. A. O. Schwarz / Schwarz Toy Bazaar / F.A.O. Schwarz "The Home of Toys"

"Frederick August Otto Schwarz, an immigrant from Westphalia who moved to New York City in 1870 to open the Schwarz Toy Bazaar at 765 Broadway." Jackson. (2010) The Encyclopedia of New York, p. 428.

“765 Broadway, the Home of Toys, opposite Stewart's store”

F. A. O. Schwarz / Schwarz Toy Bazaar / F.A.O. Schwarz "The Home of Toys"

"The first F. A. O. Schwarz shop was opened in 1870 at 765 Broadway, opposite A. T. Stewart's." Brown. (1924) Fifth Avenue Old and New, p. 186.

“Lyrique Hall in fifty-nine held Church's Heart of the Andes framed”

Lyrique Hall

"Lyrique Hall; 1859. 765 Broadway. Housed various shows, including single-picture exhibition of Frederic E. Church's 'Heart of the Andes'." Voorsanger & Howat. (2000) Art and the Empire City New York 1825-1861, p. 71.

19

“Ninth Street Show, nineteen-fifty-one, before they cleared the ground”

Ninth Street Show (60 East 9th Street exhibition)

"The 'Ninth Street Show,' which began on May 21, 1951, was the public's early exposure to this emotional nonfigurative movement." Bunyan. (1990) All Around the Town, p. 150.

20

“Ninety local artists carried canvas on their backs”

Ninth Street Show (60 East 9th Street exhibition)

"a 1951 exhibition at 60 East 9th Street in which ninty local artists selected work of their own to be displayed for three weeks, most of them carrying their contributions on their own shoulders from their studios nearby." Richard Kostelanetz. (2003) SoHo The Rise and Fall of an Artists Colony, p. 58.

21

“Castelli marked the photographs”

Ninth Street Show (60 East 9th Street exhibition)

"There Castelli marked the back of a photograph with the names of the artists whose work was pictured." Richard Kostelanetz. (2003) SoHo The Rise and Fall of an Artists Colony, p. 58.

Car. Ramp

"In the 1955 Manhattan Land Book, on 60 East 9th Street, there is written 'CAR. RAMP' as well as reference to 9 Structures; 1 Story Brick or Stone or Concrete building with Store." Bromley. 1955 Manhattan Land Book, p. 30.
