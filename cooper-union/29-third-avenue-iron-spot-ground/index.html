Every line traces one triangular lot through 177 years — an empty parcel in the 1815 Blue Book, wood-frame tenements and Wundling’s Hotel, and the 1992 Neo‑Brutalist Cooper Union dormitory clad in dark iron‑spot brick. Sourced line by line below.

I

Verse 1

01

“poured in reinforced concrete, the year of ninety-two, the dream / they picked was Neo-Brutalist, a clunky, chosen frame, / dark purplish-brown iron-spot brick-clad, forty-eight apartments, same / one hundred 93 students racked above the retail floor”

Cooper Union Residence Hall (Undergraduate Dormitory)

"The selection of Ohlhausen's clunky, Neo-Brutalist proposal" and "As realized in 1992, the building was somewhat toned down and far less aggressive, a fifteen-story, dark purplish brown iron-spot brick-clad reinforced-concrete structure housing 193 students in forty-eight apartments above retail spaces." Stern. (2006) New York 2000, p. 368.

02

“a slim faceted polygonal tower — low-key, crisp, and more / than the critics gave it credit, brutal, banal, so they swore, / but the trustee called it rational, spartan, Peter Cooper's core”

Cooper Union Residence Hall (Undergraduate Dormitory)

"The slim, faceted, polygonal tower is low-key—even bland—yet at the same time crisply detailed and tailored." and "terrible architecture: brutal in design, banal in program" and "a good New York vernacular, rationalist building, with a well-built, spartan character in the spirit of Peter Cooper." Stern. (2006) New York 2000, p. 368.

03

“Fifteen stories overlapping grids, the square and rectangle massing, / three intersecting structures where the narrow strips keep passing / neighborhood scale to the wider high-rise contemporary street wall”

Cooper Union Residence Hall (Undergraduate Dormitory)

"Overlapping grids dictated the building's square and rectangular massing. In reality three separate intersecting structures... narrow strips retain the character of the neighborhood while the wider street wall of the high-rise tower looks more contemporary." Sirefman. (2001) New York A Guide to Recent Architecture, p. 89.

04

“a triangle drawn tight between the school, the dorm, Tom Thumb, and all”

Cooper Union Residence Hall (Undergraduate Dormitory)

"The location creates a triangle between the school, the dorm and a statue of Tom Thumb." Sirefman. (2001) New York A Guide to Recent Architecture, p. 89.

05

“the base that matches Alumni Hall's own roofline just across — / then leaps”

Undergraduate Dormitory, N.Y.U. / New York University Residence Hall (Alumni Hall)

"Note that the base of the N.Y.U. building matches the roofline of its neighbors, then leaps up 16 stories to its roof that is surmounted by what appears to be a blimp hangar." Wolfe. (2003) New York 15 Walking Tours, p. 144.

Chorus

06

“southeast corner, Stuyvesant Street”

Cooper Union Residence Hall (Undergraduate Dormitory)

"embarked on the construction of its first-ever dormitory, to be located on a triangular site at 29 Third Avenue, southeast corner of Stuyvesant Street." Stern. (2006) New York 2000, p. 368.

07

“the bouwerie the Dutch designed. / Governor Peter Stuyvesant's farm — the grid laid over garden ground”

Undergraduate Dormitory, N.Y.U. / New York University Residence Hall (Alumni Hall)

"Stuyvesant Street was originally designed to be the entrance to Governor Peter Stuyvesant's farm... 'by restoring a patch of greenery to what was in the 17th century part of Stuyvesant's bouwerie, or farm.'" Wolfe. (2003) New York 15 Walking Tours, p. 144.

Perris. 1853 Perris Maps, p. 49.

1885 Robinson Atlas

1897 Bromleys Atlas

IV

Bridge

16

“Nineteen-eighty-nine — the two-story store relieved of load, / the college canvassed twenty alumni, chose the clunky mode”

Cooper Union Residence Hall (Undergraduate Dormitory)

"In 1989 Cooper Union... embarked on the construction of its first-ever dormitory... Cooper canvassed twenty alumni and faculty members... The selection of Ohlhausen's clunky, Neo-Brutalist proposal." Stern. (2006) New York 2000, p. 368.

17

“seven hundred signed against it — mediocre, no merit, they cried”

Cooper Union Residence Hall (Undergraduate Dormitory)

"700 of whom signed a petition that dismissed the design as 'a simplistic, developer-style building of mediocre quality and no architectural merit.'" Stern. (2006) New York 2000, p. 368.

18

“Three finalists, three sons of the school, each with a drafting hand, / a year and a half of debate before the trustees made their stand”

Cooper Union Residence Hall (Undergraduate Dormitory)

"The three finalists were all Cooper Union graduates: Peter Eisenman, Diane Lewis and Rolf Olhausen. A year and a half of debate ensued before Prentice & Chan, Olhausen was given the commission." Sirefman. (2001) New York A Guide to Recent Architecture, p. 89.

19

“Concrete cast, the tower rose, the party walls set into grid”

St. Mark's Bookshop

"concrete 'fish bone' structural elements conceived of as a sieve... set into the grid of the party walls of the surrounding buildings." Stern. (2006) New York 2000, p. 368.

20

“and the bookshop moved into the odd space that it hid, / St. Mark's, a former German shooting club, the service desk dead-center laid”

St. Mark's Bookshop

"the bookshop moved to its current more spacious location, a former German shooting club." Holmes. (2004) 100 New Yorker, p. 82. and "Don Zivkovic located the service desk in the center from which the staff could survey the aisles of books." Stern. (2006) New York 2000, p. 1141.

21

“the ducts like writhing intestines overhead where the panopticon was made”

St. Mark's Bookshop

"the contrast of the static shelving with the writhing, intestine-like shapes of the ductwork overhead... suggesting the Panopticon-inspired arrangements." Stern. (2006) New York 2000, p. 1141.
