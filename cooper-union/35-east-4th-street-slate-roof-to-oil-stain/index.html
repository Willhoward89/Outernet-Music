Every line traces one lot’s fall from grace — four first‑class dwellings on the 1853 Perris maps, consolidated into Everett Hall by 1885, a four‑story brick assembly hall with basement and rear extensions through 1916, torn down and reduced to a plain two‑story garage by 1930. 35 East 4th Street

"In the 1853 Perris Maps, on 35 East 4th Street, there is reference to 4 structures; Brick or Stone dwellings first class (slate or metal roof & coped) with Brick or Stone Extension and skylights lighting the top floor only, Brick or Stone dwellings first class (slate or metal roof & coped) with Framed Extension and skylights lighting the top floor only, Brick or Stone Store first class, Framed Store first class (slate or metal roof & coped)." Perris. 1853 Perris Maps, p. 60.

35 East 4th Street

"In the 1867 Dripps Plan, on 35 East 4th Street, there is reference to 3 structures; Building, Building, Building." Dripps. 1867 Dripps Plan, p. 7.

“Come the 1885 Robinson it reads 'Everett Hall,' four brick structures listed, brick and brick and brick, that's all”

Everett Hall

"In the 1885 Robinson Atlas, on 35 East 4th Street, there is written 'Everett Hall' as well as reference to 4 Structures; Brick building, Brick building, Brick building, Brick building." Robinson. 1885 Robinson Atlas, p. 12.

05

“Then 1897 Bromley refines it, one structure now defined: 'Everett Hall,' four-story brick, a basement dug and lined”

Everett Hall

"In the 1897 Bromleys Atlas, on 35 East 4th Street, there is written 'Everett Hall' as well as reference to 1 Structure; 4 Story Brick building with Basement." Bromley. 1897 Bromleys Atlas, p. 16.

06

“And 1916 Bromley logs it four floors, brick, and true: one story of Brick Extension where the older footprint grew, a two-story Brick Extension reaching further toward the rear, 'Everett Hall' with basement”

Everett Hall

"In the 1916 Atlas of the Borough of Manhattan, on 35 East 4th Street, there is written 'Everett Hall' as well as reference to 1 Structure; 4 Story Brick building with Basement and 1 Story Brick Extension and 2 Story Brick Extension." G W Bromley & Co. 1916 Atlas of the Borough of Manhattan, p. 34.
