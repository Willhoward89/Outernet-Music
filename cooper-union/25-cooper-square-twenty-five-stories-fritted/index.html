Every line traces one lot through over 170 years — ten structures on the 1853 Perris maps, a gas station by 1955, Robert Moses’s slum‑clearance plan shelved in 1960, and Carlos Zapata’s twenty‑one‑story fritted‑glass wedge that rose in 2009 — nicknamed the Shark’s Fin, the Splinter, the Iceberg. 07

“Fritted glass recalling Gehry, the facade that billows out, away from off the Bowery as it rises... intimate at sidewalk, though it towers twenty-one, a path splits through the lobby to the terrace and the sun”

Cooper Square Hotel

"fritted glass recalling Frank Gehry's IAC Building (59) in Chelsea... the milky white facade billows away from the Bowery as it rises... it is surprisingly intimate at sidewalk level... a path that splits apart the lobby and its own bar." Hill. (2011) Contemporary New York Architecture, p. 54.

08

“Around the corner Fifth Street, a garden set at grade, the backyard of the East Village, that's the myth they made. Interiors by Citterio... where decades back a hotel here would never cross the mind”

Cooper Square Hotel

"Around the corner on 5th Street is another entrance and a garden terrace at grade. Purportedly these outdoor spaces were inspired by the backyards common to the East Village... Interiors by Italian designer Antonio Citterio... where decades earlier the idea of a hotel would hardly have been entertained." Hill. (2011) Contemporary New York Architecture, p. 54.

“Drop to eighteen-fifty-three... ten structures on the lot, second-class the record set — brick or stone with stores beneath, slate or metal, roof not coped, framed dwellings with the stores under, first-class stores that sloped”

"In the 1853 Perris Maps, on 25 Cooper Square, there is reference to 10 structures; a Brick or stone dwelling second class (slate or metal roof not coped) with stores under... a framed dwelling with stores under, a framed brick or stone store first class... a brick or stone store first class." Perris. 1853 Perris Maps, p. 48.

15

“By sixty-four the Bowery south was unkempt, work-a-day, an unnamed hand had painted it, that vital gray decay, no prominent buildings, no clarity, just the truthful throng — the Prints Division holds it now”

Cooper Square (1864 view)

"The more impressionistic but also more truthful view of the Bowery looking south from Cooper Square was painted by an unnamed artist in 1864. Although it lacks the prominent buildings and the visual clarity of the preceding view, it is unusually successful in conveying a sense of the unkempt vitality of the Bowery and of work-a-day New York generally. Both views courtesy of the Prints Division, New York Public Library." Spann. (1981) The New Metropolis, p. 93.

“Then fifty-six, Moses swung the Slum Clearance dream, twenty-one-and-a-half acres slated, whole quarter of the scheme — Houston up to St. Mark's, the Bowery to the Second, twenty-four-hundred tenants gone... By nineteen-sixty shelved”

Cooper Square (Title 1 project)

"The year 1956 saw... the proposal, issued by Robert Moses's Slum Clearance Committee, for a major Title 1 project... a 21.5-acre area bounded on the south by East Houston Street, on the west by the Bowery, on the north by St. Mark's Place and on the east by Second Avenue... 2,400 tenants... were to be displaced... In 1960, in response to the protest, the newly established Housing and Redevelopment Board shelved the plan." Stern. (1995) New York 1960, p. 257.
