Inspired by old‑school hip‑hop

Every line traces one Broadway block through three centuries of ownership — a Dutch land grant, Baron Poelnitz’s farm, Sailors’ Snug Harbor, A.T. Stewart’s Iron Palace, and the nine‑story holdout that left Wanamaker’s Renaissance arcade unfinished for twenty years. Sourced line by line below.

Verse 1

01

“Burnham drew the elevation, nineteen-oh-four the founding year”

Wanamaker's Department Store Annex

"756-770 Broadway, 1904-10 and 1926 - D.H. Burnham & Company." Hennessy. (2020) Walking Broadway, p. 85.

02

“Three-story arches Renaissance in ranks along the street, / Inscribed grid across the white facade”

Wanamaker's Department Store Annex

"Ranks of powerful, three-story arches march along the street and a distinct, inscribed grid runs through this massive white facade." Dunlap. (1990) On Broadway, p. 102.

03

“Southwest corner missing, nine-story holdout scar, / Snug Harbor held the lease, the lot line drawn bizarre”

770 Broadway

"Wanamaker's was unable to obtain possession of all the necessary land. The property is owned by Sailors' Snug Harbor, a refuge for 'aged, decrepit and worn-out sailors'… the cost of acquiring possession of the newly built large nine-story structure at the corner of Broadway and 8th Street was prohibitive." Alpern & Durst. (1984) Architectural Holdouts, p. 43.

04

“Built around the gap for twenty years, unfinished, incomplete, / Nineteen-twenty-six they closed the wound”

770 Broadway

"The new store was built around the holdout… and it remained in that unfinished state until 1926, when the leasehold was finally acquired and the store building completed." Alpern & Durst. (1984) Architectural Holdouts, p. 43.

05

“Seventy-four-twenty-two pipes deep, thirteen hundred seats”

Wanamaker's Department Store Annex

"an enormous sky-lit rotunda filled the center of the new store. In addition to selling just about any type of merchandise imaginable, there were restaurants, retiring rooms, a theater seating 1,300, and a formidable organ with 7,422 pipes." Hennessy. (2020) Walking Broadway, p. 85.

06

“Now it's dot-com code where dry goods fold”

Wanamaker's...On The Air!

"offices upstairs house dot-com powerhouses Facebook, AOL, and the Huffington Post." Young & Meyers. (2016) Adventures in Old New York, p. 189.

07

“Sixteen stories, fireproof steel behind the brick veneer”

John Wanamaker Department Store

"In the 1916 Atlas of the Borough of Manhattan, on 770 Broadway, there is written 'John Wanamaker Department Store' as well as reference to… 14 Story Brick Building with Store and Elevators and Fire proof building (steel construction)." G W Bromley & Co. 1916 Atlas of the Borough of Manhattan, p. 41.

III

Verse 2

10

“Seventeen-sixty-six, the governor called it Minto, / Twenty-two-and-a-half acres, Eighth to Tenth, the perimeter window”

Baron Frederick Charles Hans Bruno Poelnitz estate

"The most highly cultivated country place near the city was that of Baron Frederick Charles Hans Bruno Poelnitz, comprising 22½ acres of land situated on the present Broadway between Eighth and Tenth Streets… This place had been purchased in 1766 by Lieut. Governor Elliot and by him was called 'Minto.'" Smith. (1889) The city of New York in the year of Washington's Inauguration, 1789, p. 51.

11

“Baron Poelnitz, fancy farming, eighty-nine he tried to sell, / Choicest fruit trees, flowering shrubs, the richest soil would tell”

Baron Frederick Charles Hans Bruno Poelnitz estate

"in 1789 was devoted to fancy farming by Baron Poelnitz, who offered it for sale in that year. The advertisement of it stated that it was about two miles from the city and abounded with a greater variety of the choicest fruit trees and flowering shrubs than perhaps any other place in the state, while it possessed the richest soil of any estate on Manhattan Island." Smith. (1889) The city of New York in the year of Washington's Inauguration, 1789, p. 51.

12

“Randall bought it, seventeen-ninety, five thousand pounds the deed, / Died eighteen-oh-one and willed it all for sailors in their need”

Baron Frederick Charles Hans Bruno Poelnitz estate

"In 1790 it was sold to Robert R. Randall for £5000 and by his will in 1801 it was devoted to the purposes of the Sailors' Snug Harbor." Smith. (1889) The city of New York in the year of Washington's Inauguration, 1789, p. 51.

13

“Assento Angola, sixteen-forties Dutch, / Lot fourteen, a hundred seventy feet west, Sand Hill Road and such”

Assento Angola

"Assento Angola (Liber Patents, II: 124, Albany). It was designated No. 14. It was situated about 170 ft. west of the present Fourth Ave., on both sides of 8th St., north of the Sand Hill Road." Stokes. (1922) The Iconography of Manhattan Island Volume Four, p. 266.

14

“Pieter Tamboer, lot number four, the crossway at the corner ran”

Pieter Tamboer

"Pieter Tamboer (Liber Patents, II: 121, Albany). It was designated No. 4, and was situated at the north-west corner of the old Sand Hill Road (the Cross-way), now the corner of 8th St. and Fourth Ave." Stokes. (1922) The Iconography of Manhattan Island Volume Four, p. 265.

IV

Bridge

15

“July fifteen, fifty-six, the Iron Palace burns, / Two days out of control, a fiery farewell, every girder turns”

Iron Palace

"the old cast-iron building, which occupied the entire block between 9th and 10th streets, fell victim on July 15, 1956, to one of New York's most spectacular conflagrations, blazing out of control for two days in a fiery farewell to the old merchant prince." Wolfe. (2003) New York 15 Walking Tours, p. 222.

16

“Stewart built it eighteen-sixty-eight, the largest cast-iron frame”

A. T. Stewart's

"A. T. Stewart's grand 'Iron Palace' department store, so nicknamed for being the largest cast-iron building of its day when it was constructed in 1868." Young & Meyers. (2016) Adventures in Old New York, p. 189.

17

“Wanamaker bought it ninety-six but fire don't check a name”

Wanamaker's

"In 1896, Stewart's was purchased by its chief rival, Wanamaker's, which took up shop inside the Iron Palace." Young & Meyers. (2016) Adventures in Old New York, p. 189.

18

“They named it Stewart House in brick where nothing rhymes the same”

Stewart House

"The apartment building that now occupies the site of the 'Cast-Iron Palace' is named Stewart House to recall the original merchant prince." Wolfe. (2003) New York 15 Walking Tours, p. 222.

19

“The store closed fifty-four”

Wanamaker's

"Wanamakers continued in business until 1954, when the store closed." Hennessy. (2020) Walking Broadway, p. 85.

20

“the foundation orphaned underground”

Sailors' Snug Harbour

"The building used for the 'Sailors' Snug Harbour' was the Randall mansion, situated on Broadway between 8th and 9th Sts., which, with the property attached to it, had already 'become of great value.'" Stokes. (1926) The Iconography of Manhattan Island Volume Five, p. 1672.
