726 BroadwaySeven‑Twenty‑Six
(The Scent of Church)

Inspired by proto rap jazz poetry

Every line traces one lot through a century of disguises — the Unitarian Church of the Messiah, reborn as the Broadway Athenaeum and a string of gaslit theaters, burned in 1884, rebuilt as a faux London street with a Bishop’s Gate facade, then a gymnasium, then Wanamaker’s warehouse. “The scent of church lingers about it still.” Sourced line by line below.

Chorus

08

“opposite Waverly, where the church and the stage meet”

Broadway Athenaeum

"The Broadway Athenaeum (1865), located at 728-730 Broadway, opposite Waverly Place, was probably the city's first postwar playhouse and certainly one of its most unusual." Stern. (1999) New York 1880, p. 663.

“'the scent of church lingers about it' like it always did”

The Old London Gate

"You may paint, you may fresco the place as you will / But the scent of church lingers about it still." Dunlap. (2004) From Abyssinian to Zion, p. 48.

Verse 2

10

“Drop to eighteen-thirty, La Grange marble and stone — the Colonnade Hotel stood, an entrance all its own”

Colonnade Hotel

"Site of Old London Street, 1887; Unitarian Church of the Messiah... Colonnade Hotel, 1830-33 (an entrance to La Grange Terrace on Lafayette Street)." Dunlap. (1990) On Broadway, p. 101.

11

“Sing Sing quarried Westchester marble, cut and set the frame, Corinthian and coped where La Grange Terrace came”

Colonnade Hotel

"It seems that the convicts at Sing Sing not only quarried the Westchester marble for the buildings, but they cut and prepared it as well... the two-story Corinthian colonnade evokes the classical grandeur of Greece's golden era." Hemp. (1975) New York Enclaves, p. 28.

12

“the fifty-three Perris map read the mortar clear: Unitarian Church, Presbyterian Church both were here, brick and stone dwellings, first class, slate and metal crowned, coped along the roofline, second-class the framed extension found”

Presbyterian Church

"In the 1853 Perris Maps, on 726 Broadway, there is written 'Unitarian Church, Presbyterian Church' as well as reference to 10 Structures; Brick or Stone dwellings first class (slate or metal roof & coped)... Brick or stone dwellings second class (slate or metal roof not coped) with Framed Extension." Perris. (1853) Perris Maps, p. 61.

13

“Erected thirty-nine, the house of prayer went up: Unitarian Church of the Messiah filled the cup”

Unitarian Church of the Messiah

"Unitarian Church of the Messiah (second site). 543-3 Site: 728-730 Broadway. Erected 1839 (Greenleaf's Hist. of the Churches, 376; cf. Liber Deeds, CCCLXXXV: 152); sold 1864." Stokes. (1918) The Iconography of Manhattan Island Volume Three, p. 937.

14

“'Even in some future age this pulpit may be a stage' — Oakey Hall's prophecy set in the mortar's age”

Broadway Athenaeum

"a young student, A. Oakey Hall, later mayor during the heyday of Boss Tweed, had recited a prophetic couplet when the building was still a church: 'Even in some future age / This pulpit may be a stage.'" Stern. (1999) New York 1880, p. 663.

15

“Sold in sixty-four, and A. T. Stewart bought the shell, J. H. Hackett cut the tower down, the Athenaeum fell”

Broadway Athenaeum

"In 1865 the department store entrepreneur Alexander T. Stewart, working with the architect J. H. Hackett, converted the former Unitarian Church of the Messiah (1838) into a theater... The tower of the church was removed in 1869." Stern. (1999) New York 1880, p. 663.

16

“the sixty-seven Dripps plan marked the N.Y. Theatre frame”

New York Theatre (N.Y. Theatre)

"In the 1867 Dripps Plan, on 726 Broadway, there is written 'N.Y. Theatre' as well as reference to 9 Structures; Building, Building, Building, Building, Building, Building, Building, Building, Building." Dripps. (1867) Dripps Plan, p. 7.

17

“Worrell Sisters staged the gaslight rescue on the track, Under the Gaslight — the cliff-hanger never looked back”

Worrell Sisters' Theatre

"In 1867 at the Worrell Sisters' Theatre, 728 Broadway at Waverly Place, a play by Augustin Daly called 'Under the Gaslight' left audiences breathless... gave the impression of a train speeding across the stage seconds after the rescue of someone who had been tied to the track." Miller. (1990) Greenwich Village and how it got that way, p. 104.

Daly's Broadway Theatre

"Globe Theatre 1870; Nixon's Amphitheatre 1871; Daly's Fifth Ave. Theatre 1873; Fox's Broadway Theatre 1874; later in that year the Globe again; Bryant's Opera House; the National Theatre 1875; Globe 1878; New Theatre Comique (Harrigan & Hart) 1881."
