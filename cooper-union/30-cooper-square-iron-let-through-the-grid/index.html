04

“I read it plain — a building 'made of railroad traces,'
modified railroad ties from the ironworks Cooper places,
I-beam construction, one of the first to bear the load,
common now, but back then it was writ into the code”

The Building Made of Railroad Traces

"The building was one of the first to use steel I-beam construction, a common feature of most buildings today. The beams his building employed were actually modified railroad ties from the same ironworks that Cooper used for regular track production." [Young & Meyers. "(2016) Adventures in Old New York," p. 190.

05

“Peter Cooper, rags-to-riches, bearded fanciful mind,
built a steam engine early, left the ironworks behind,
took the beams from the same mill the regular track was drawn,
laid them in the frame so the whole thing carried on”

The Building Made of Railroad Traces

"Peter Cooper (1791-1883) was one of New York's most famous inventors, a fanciful and bearded genius with a rags-to-riches tale, who constructed, among other things, one of America's first steam engines." [Young & Meyers. "(2016) Adventures in Old New York," p. 190.

06

“Base to cornice, brownstone kin to the Italianate scheme,
a classical adaptation to modern function — a machine
much in the spirit of the Rundbogenstil down on Lafayette,
strong enough to hold the open square, no felicity in the set”

Cooper Union

"The design is strong enough to hold the space... Not much in the way of felicitous detail in the heavy, simple, Italianate, brownstone mass, a classical adaptation to modern function much in the spirit of the Rundbogenstil Astor Library around the corner on Lafayette Street." [Morrone. "(1994) Architectural Guidebook," p. 95.

07

“The front is on the south side, an island of stone and mass,
Cooper Square laid before it like a coordinate of glass”

Cooper Union

"The front of the building is actually on the south side. Cooper Union forms an island... The open space in front of the main entrance is called Cooper Square." [Morrone. "(1994) Architectural Guidebook," p. 95.

08

“And out before the entrance, cast in eighteen ninety-four,
Saint-Gaudens' bronze of Peter Cooper, seated, robed and sure —
Stanford White's marble canopy, feminine, enveloping frame,
against the manly bearded figure, holding down its name”

Peter Cooper monument

"Augustus Saint-Gaudens's Peter Cooper monument (1894). The bronze figure is seated on a marble-and-granite base with canopy by Stanford White. There is a lovely contrast between White's feminine enveloping scheme and the manly, bearded, robust figure." [Morrone. "(1994) Architectural Guidebook," p. 95.
