2 Taras Shevchenko PlaceNine to Zero to One

Every line traces one lot through nearly 190 years — a house of worship built for $16,000 in 1838, passed within five years from St. Timothy’s Church to the Sixth Street Baptist Church to the Church of the Redemption, replaced by nine crowded structures on the 1853 Perris maps, wiped to an empty lot by 1955, and rebuilt in 1956 for the Order of St. Basil the Great — now St. George Parochial Catholic School. Verse 2

04

“a house of worship raised for sixteen thousand on the block”

St. Timothy's Church

"In the course of that year, a house of worship was erected on Sixth street, near the Bowery, at a cost of $16,000, which was finished and consecrated, October 3d, 1838." Greenleaf. (1846) A History of the Churches of all Denominations in the City of New York, p. 102.

05

“Then the minister was taken, death before the plaster dried, their rising hopes were blasted, and the whole thing broke inside”

St. Timothy's Church

"But before this took place, the church was suddenly deprived of its minister by death. This blasted their rising hopes, and broke them up." Greenleaf. (1846) A History of the Churches of all Denominations in the City of New York, p. 102.

06

“A heavy debt lay on the edifice they could not discharge, suffered it to be sold, the mortgage swallowed it at large. By eighteen forty gone, bought for fourteen thousand clean”

St. Timothy's Church

"A heavy debt lay upon the church edifice, which the congregation could not discharge, and they suffered it to be sold under foreclosure of mortgage. It was bought for $14,000, by the 'Sixth Street Baptist Church,' and 'St. Timothy's Church' became extinct. This took place in the year 1840." Greenleaf. (1846) A History of the Churches of all Denominations in the City of New York, p. 102.

07

“Sixth Street Baptist Church, one fifty-six between — Rev. John O. Choules preaching, April, forty, organized, dismissed from Bethel's fold”

Sixth Street Baptist Church

"Sixth Street Baptist Church. This church was organized in April, 1840, with one hundred and fifty-six members, who were dismissed for this purpose from that portion of the Bethel Church... Of this new church, the Rev. John O. Choules became pastor... they had purchased a house of worship in Sixth street, near the Bowery, for $14,000." Greenleaf. (1846) A History of the Churches of all Denominations in the City of New York, p. 268.

08

“The location, so they judged, was rather unfavorable to keep, so they sold it forty-three, all debts paid, the balance deep”

Sixth Street Baptist Church

"The location of the church was thought, however, to be on some accounts rather unfavorable; and in the early part of 1843, they sold the building to an Episcopal Society (the Church of the Redemption), paid all their debts, and had a balance on hand." Greenleaf. (1846) A History of the Churches of all Denominations in the City of New York, p. 268.

09

“to the Church of the Redemption, formed November twenty-eight, eleven thousand, forty-five members, Isaac Pardee at the gate”

Church of the Redemption

"it was purchased for $11,000, by an Episcopal Congregation, formed November 28th, 1842, and styled 'The Church of the Redemption.' This church has about forty-five members. Rev. Isaac Pardee is Rector." Greenleaf. (1846) A History of the Churches of all Denominations in the City of New York, p. 103.
