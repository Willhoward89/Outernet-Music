“eighteen sixty-seven, that's the cornerstone I need”

First Ukrainian Assembly of God, formerly the Metropolitan Savings Bank

"The Metropolitan Savings Bank built this fireproof commercial building in 1867." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 246.

02

“rusticated stone, boldly cut, a masonry skin over a frame of iron bone”

First Ukrainian Assembly of God, formerly the Metropolitan Savings Bank

"The elegant Second Empire masonry skin is supported by a structural iron frame... as do the boldly rusticated base and the ornate cornice." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 246.

03

“Second Empire idiom, Carl Pfeiffer drew the plan, dynamically massed on a relatively small span”

Metropolitan Savings Bank

"Pfeiffer handled the Second Empire idiom in a sophisticated manner, creating a dynamically massed structure on a relatively small site." Dolkart & Postal. (2009) NYC Landmarks, p. 64.

04

“Five bays fronting the Square, eight down East Seventh's line, horizontal band at every floor to make the whole thing align”

First Ukrainian Assembly of God, formerly the Metropolitan Savings Bank

"presents two impressive façades: one with five bays on Cooper Square, the other with eight bays on East 7th Street. A horizontal band at each floor unifies the refined composition." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 246.

05

“Pilasters frame the windows, vertical against the flat, Corinthian columns flanking where the central archway sat”

First Ukrainian Assembly of God, formerly the Metropolitan Savings Bank

"The vertical emphasis of the pilasters framing the windows contrasts with an otherwise horizontal composition... A handsome doorway, framed by a central arch and flanked by Corinthian columns." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 246.

06

“Climb it to the mansard — dormers set in rows, segmental arches crowning where the roofline goes”

First Ukrainian Assembly of God, formerly the Metropolitan Savings Bank

"A series of dormer windows, crowned with segmental arches, appears in the mansard roof." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 246.

07

“the fireproof shell is stone, but the load is iron below”

Cooper Square Assembly of God/originally The Metropolitan Savings Bank

"Marble parallel to the then-current cast-iron world: such material denoted class (cast iron was used to gain elaboration inexpensively)." AIA. (1988) AIA Edition 3, p. 165.

Verse 2

10

“Perris Maps say two structures where the frame would later spawn: brick or stone dwellings, second class in kind, slate or metal roofs, not coped — stores underneath, you'll find”

Metropolitan Savings Bank

"In the 1853 Perris Maps, on 61 Cooper Square, there is reference to 2 structures; a Brick or stone dwelling second class (slate or metal roof not coped) with stores under, a Brick or stone dwelling second class (slate or metal roof not coped) with stores under." Perris. 1853 Perris Maps, p. 49.

11

“Peter Cooper buying parcels, eighteen thirty-nine his goal — sixteen oddly shaped lots he gathered through the years, July of eighteen fifty-two the last one clears”

Metropolitan Savings Bank

"It had begun to take shape as early as December, 1839... buying from different owners the other sixteen oddly shaped parcels into which the estate of Nicholas W. Stuyvesant had divided the block. And it had already taken specific form when, in July, 1852, he finally bought the last lot." Mack. (1949) Peter Cooper, p. 244.

12

“built to serve the largely German inhabitants near, till Pfeiffer's marble rose in sixty-seven”

Metropolitan Savings Bank, now the First Ukrainian Evangelical Pentecostal Church

"Built to serve the largely German inhabitants of this East Village neighborhood, the Metropolitan Savings Bank is a significant example of French Second Empire design." Postal & Dolkart. (2009) Guide to New York City Landmarks, p. 65.

“Metropolitan holds sixty-eight years, then the deed transfers belief”

First Ukrainian Assembly of God, formerly the Metropolitan Savings Bank

"The Metropolitan Savings Bank built this fireproof commercial building in 1867 and occupied it for 68 years. Since 1937, it has been owned and used for religious purposes by the Ukrainian Church." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 246.

First Ukrainian Assembly of God

"Mammon was the first god served at 9 East 7th Street, built in 1867 as the Metropolitan Savings Bank, by Carl Pfeiffer. It was purchased by the Ukrainian Assembly of God congregation in 1937." Dunlap. (2004) From Abyssinian to Zion, p. 79.

“First Ukrainian Assembly of God takes the iron frame... the marble building purchased”

Metropolitan Savings Bank, now the First Ukrainian Evangelical Pentecostal Church

"In 1937 the marble building was purchased by the First Ukrainian Assembly of God, and it has since served as a church." Dolkart & Postal. (2009) NYC Landmarks, p. 64.
