Every line traces one address through a century of stone — an 1850s coal yard, the 1904 Carnegie library McKim, Mead & White raised for Tompkins Square, the immigrant readers who filled it in seven languages, and two renovations that carried it into the present. Sourced line by line below.

"NEW YORK PUBLIC LIBRARY, TOMPKINS SQUARE BRANCH 1904; 1960; 1996 331 EAST 10TH STREET, MANHATTAN." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 497.

Coal Yard

"Silver - 201 192 187, <Façade Color>_Almond - 238 218 197, _Granite, <Façade Material>_Limestone." LPC. (1904) Tompkins Square Park, p. 1.

03

“nineteen-oh-four the cornerstone read plain, / McKim, Mead & White drew the plan”

New York Public Library, Tompkins Square Branch

"Characteristic of the Carnegie libraries, this classically-inspired, midblock, three-story, three-bay building is organized along a vertical plan. Its symmetrical design features an offset entryway." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 497.

04

“a fanlight over transom, a sidebay door”

Coal Yard

"Fanlight, Transom, Arched Lintel, Wooden Pilasters, Sidebay Entry, 3 Bays." LPC. (1904) Tompkins Square Park, p. 1.

05

“Rusticated pilasters, foliated capitals, incised stone and molded sill”

Coal Yard

"Incised Stone, Molded Sills, Arched Lintels... Rusticated Pilasters... Foliated Capitals." LPC. (1904) Tompkins Square Park, p. 1.

06

“second of twelve he would design, / third of twenty Carnegie built”

Tompkins Square

"This building was the second of 12 branch libraries designed by McKim, Mead & White, and the third of 20 branches built in Manhattan with Andrew Carnegie's gift." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 497.

07

“a swag and shield, the city's seal, the rosettes cut to outlast / Aldus Manutius, Christophe Plantin”

Tompkins Square

"Above the second story windows, a shield incorporates the seal of the City of New York, and rosettes commemorate Aldus Manutius and Cristophe Plaintin Press, major figures in the history of printing." Dolkart & Postal. (2009) NYC Landmarks, p. 69.

08

“egg and dart, the dentiled cornice reaching for the sky alone”

Coal Yard

"Dentiled Cornice, Swag and Shield, Egg and Dart, Foliated Capitals." LPC. (1904) Tompkins Square Park, p. 1.

Verse 2

09

“eighteen fifty-three, the Perris hand / wrote Coal Yard on three thirty-one — zero structures”

Coal Yard

"In the 1853 Perris Maps, on 331 East 10th Street, there is written 'Coal Yard' as well as reference to 0 Structures." Perris. 1853 Perris Maps, p. 47.

10

“Eighteen sixty-seven, Dripps confirms it — nothing risen yet, / zero structures on the plate”

Coal Yard

"In the 1867 Dripps Plan, on 331 East 10th Street, there is reference to 0 structures." Dripps. 1867 Dripps Plan, p. 8.

11

“Then eighteen eighty-five, Robinson — two brick buildings stand”

Coal Yard

"In the 1885 Robinson Atlas, on 331 East 10th Street, there is reference to 2 structures; a Brick building, a brick building." Robinson. 1885 Robinson Atlas, p. 10.

12

“eighteen ninety-seven, Bromley: five-story brick, a doubled span”

Coal Yard

"In the 1897 Bromleys Atlas, on 331 East 10th Street, there is reference to 2 structures; a 5 story brick building, a 5 story brick building." Bromley. 1897 Bromleys Atlas, p. 17.

13

“nineteen-oh-four, the same year the General Slocum burned the shore, / overnight the German quarter left, and Slavic, Jewish, Irish poured / into the block”

Tompkins Square Park Library

"1904. General Slocum disaster. Overnight the German population moves out to be replaced by Jewish, Slavic, African and Irish immigrants. Tompkins Square Branch Library built as part of the Carnegie library system opens to serve them." Patterson. (2007) Resistance, p. 239.

14

“the Tompkins Square Park Library opened wide, / books in seven languages set to serve the newly-arrived tide”

Tompkins Square Park Library

"The Tompkins Square Park Library was built in 1904 at 331 East Tenth Street, featuring books in seven languages to serve the immigrants." Kayton. (1999) Radical Walking Tours of New York City, p. 93.

New York Public Library, Tompkins Square Branch

“the Thompkins Square Branch re-coded down the hall”

Thompkins Square Branch

"NOTES ON THE PLATES Thompkins Square Branch, 331 E. 10th St., New York, N.Y. Constructed 1903-05; cost, $75,141. Extant." Blom. (1973) McKim Mead & White 1879-1915, p. 67.

17

“Then nineteen ninety-six — RKT&B strips the flush to make the accessible”

New York Public Library, Tompkins Square Branch

"In 1996, the building was made accessible by RKT&B Architects, who also renovated the interior." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 497.

18

“The Polish stacks that made it famous in the war years… the Tompkins Square Branch renovated into itself”

Tompkins Square Branch

"During the early twentieth century, the Tompkins Square Branch was known throughout the city for its Polish book collection, becoming a center of Polish intellectual life during World War II." Spielvogel. (2016) The Landmarks of New York, p. 497.

Tompkins Square

"Tompkins Square, Man. 331 East 10 St. Hungarian, Polish." Slocum & Todd. (1939) A Key to New York, p. 5.
