# 1–3 Union Square West

*Stone Threshold at the Square's Edge* — an annotated, sourced text on R. H. Robertson's Lincoln
Building at the acute north-west corner of East Fourteenth Street and Union Square West: a
nine-storey commercial block of 1889–90 that combined a metal interior skeleton with masonry bearing
walls, and so sits exactly on the threshold between the load-bearing wall and the true steel-cage
skyscraper.

Eleven sections and a preface, moving from the empty angle of the 1815 Blue Book through the
building's structure, its tripartite composition, its Indiana limestone and terra-cotta ornament —
griffins, Byzantine capitals, human and lion heads — to the cornice of forty little windows that
crowns it, and on to its landmark designation in 1988.

Every quoted passage is highlighted and colour-coded by source. **All 62 quotes are clickable** —
each opens its scanned source page in a panel beside the text.

## Sources

13 works are cited across 13 pages, **every one on file**.

| Source | Quotes |
|---|---|
| LPC, *Union Square*, 1890, p. 1 | 39 |
| Spielvogel, *The Landmarks of New York*, 2016, p. 346 | 9 |
| Stern, *New York 1880*, 1999, p. 442 | 2 |
| Postal & Dolkart, *Guide to New York City Landmarks*, 2009, p. 76 | 2 |
| Hennessy, *Walking Broadway*, 2020, p. 97 | 2 |
| Sackersdorff, AIA, Wolfe, Stern *New York 2000*, and four atlas plates | 1 each |

The distribution is unusual: **two sources carry 48 of the 62 quotes.** A single LPC record card
supplies every ornament and material term in sections III–VIII — 39 highlights, all opening the same
card. That is correct rather than a fault, but it means those highlights show the whole card rather
than their own line on it.

Notes for the record:

- **Twelve of the thirteen scans are shared with other documents in the series.** Five come from the
  granite document (AIA, Spielvogel, Wolfe, Hennessy, Postal & Dolkart), five from 31 Union Square
  West (Sackersdorff, Stern 2000, and the 1897, 1916 and 1955 atlas plates), and Stern *New York
  1880* p. 442 from 33 East 17th Street. Only the LPC card and the 1930 plate were supplied new.
- **The LPC card and the 1930 plate each closed a gap in another document too** — the card completes
  the granite document's citation for "Granite Bulkheads" at this address, and the 1930 plate is the
  page 31 Union Square West needs for its 17-storey entry.
- **The 1930 and 1955 land-book scans are both headed "Part of Section 3 — Plate 43."** They are
  different editions of the same sheet, not duplicates: the 1930 shows the Central Mercantile
  Building at block 820 where the 1955 shows J. C. Penney. The citation page numbers, 53 and 43, are
  the volumes' own sequential pages, not the plate numbers.
- **The sources disagree on the date.** The text carries 1885, 1889 and 1890 for the same building,
  from Stern, Hennessy and Stern *New York 2000* respectively. All are reproduced as printed.
- **Quote 20, the second "Indiana Limestone", was not in the supplied citation chart** — the chart
  listed the term once where the text uses it twice, for base and façade. It is assigned to the same
  LPC card, which does list Indiana Limestone twice, under Base Material and under Façade Material.

To replace or add a scan, drop it into `assets/scans/` named exactly as the citation reads and
rebuild.
