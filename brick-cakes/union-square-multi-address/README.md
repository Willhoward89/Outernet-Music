# Union Square — multi-address

*The Palimpsest of Union Square's Northern Crown: An Architectural and Social Genealogy* — the first
document in the series to cover the square itself rather than a single building, and the first to
range across several addresses in one text.

Four opening chapters trace the square from sand hill to civic commons, through its patrician
heyday, the commercial invasion of the 1860s, and its second life as the city's political
amphitheatre. Six building sections then work around the northern rim, and a coda reads the whole as
one stratified text in masonry.

Every quoted passage is highlighted and colour-coded by source. **68 of the 74 quotes are
clickable**; the remaining six are sourced but await their record cards (see below).

**Repository path:** `brick-cakes/union-square-multi-address/`

## How it reads

**The citation chart is on the left**, as in the recent documents. The photograph rail — empty for
now — sits on the right.

- **Left — the citation chart.** It follows you as you scroll, and jumps to the matching entry when
  you hover a highlight. Click a number to go to that quote in the text, or *scan* to open the page.
  It divides into the document's eleven sections, so the chart reads as a walk around the square.
- **Centre — the text.** 74 highlighted quotes.

The side column is sticky and stacks above the text below ~1360px.

Notes for the record:

- **Two record cards are still missing** — LPC *Union Square* 1893 (the Decker Building) and 1908
  (the Everett Building). Six highlights depend on them: `Brick, Limestone, Terra Cotta`, `Tile
  Roof`, `Islamic Star Roundels`, `Terra Cotta, Marble`, `Terra Cotta, Polychrome Terra Cotta` and
  `Classical-Revival`. All six are sourced and colour-coded; they simply have no scan to open yet.
  Drop the two cards into `assets/scans/` under those exact names and rebuild to reach 74 of 74.
- **One page carries the founding narrative.** Lockwood p. 167 supplies seven consecutive quotes —
  Brevoort's farm, the 1807 regulation, the aldermen's "ventilators" report and the 1836 *Herald*
  prediction — and Lockwood contributes 16 quotes in all, the most of any author.
- **Lockwood p. 169 is a photographic plate**, captioned "Union Square, ca. 1855". The quote assigned
  to it ("Not a store is to be seen…") is not printed on the plate, so that highlight opens the
  photograph rather than its text. The page number may be off by one or two in the source chart.
- **Two different books are called *New York Then and Now*** — Watson & Gillon 1976 p. 80 and Reiss
  2019 p. 76. They are separate works with overlapping subject matter, filed under separate names;
  they sit adjacent in the source panel and look alike.
- **Eight of the 74 quotes were resolved from the series index rather than the supplied chart**, then
  confirmed against it with no disagreements.
- **The text preserves its sources' own errors**, as printed: *Democratic Earn political machine*,
  *founded m 1871*, *Io tad buildings*, *the chief motifs of which arc*, *bur also because*.

To replace or add a scan, drop it into `assets/scans/` named exactly as the citation reads and
rebuild.

```bash
# open http://localhost:8000
```
