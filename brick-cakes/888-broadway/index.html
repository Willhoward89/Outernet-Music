The corner that remembers everything, a biography of 888 Broadway, from meadow to marketplace

I. An Overture on Place and Memory

There are certain addresses in Manhattan that function less as coordinates than as compressed archives — sites where the sediment of successive eras lies layered one atop another, awaiting only a patient excavation to yield their meanings. V. The Bazaar of the World

Long before the modern emporium proclaimed its globalized taste, the building already harbored a merchant devoted to the exotic and the far-flung — a Gilded Age foretaste of the planetary sourcing that would one day define the site. IX. Coda

To contemplate 888 Broadway in full is thus to witness the entire cycle of the American metropolis compressed into a single corner: nature yielding to industry, industry to domesticity, domesticity to commerce, and commerce, finally, to a kind of aestheticized nostalgia that curates the past even as it profits from it. The estate, the coal yard, the tragedian's house of grief, the merchant's temple, the immigrant's triumphant emporium, and the vertical village of ateliers and kitchens are not separate stories but one continuous narrative — the story, in the end, of how a city remembers and forgets itself, all at once, upon the same few square feet of hallowed and heavily trafficked ground.
