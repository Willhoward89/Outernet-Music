Chapter One: When the Land Was Empty as a Playground at Night

A long, long time ago, this little spot of land was completely empty! Way back in 1815, if you had walked by, (you wouldn't have seen a single building — not one)! It was just a quiet, open patch waiting for something exciting to happen.

Little by little, buildings began to sprout up like mushrooms after the rain. By 1853, there were (cozy homes and a couple of little shops) sitting right here! Then, in 1867, a grand building called (the Everett House stood proudly on this corner). People even took an old-timey photograph of (the Everett House, which had been built way back in 1854)!

Chapter Two: A Wonderful Building Pops Out of the Ground

Then came the most exciting part! In 1881, a very clever architect named (William Schickel built a brand-new building right here). He built it for a company called (the Century Company, which made two famous magazines right inside those walls — one called The Century Magazine and one called St. Nicholas Magazine)! The grown-ups loved reading The Century, but (St. Nicholas was written just for kids) like you!

The building was named the Century Building, and here's a fun secret — it was (built as a bit of a gamble by a rich family's company called Arnold Constable, hoping people would want to rent space inside). That family was super-duper rich, because (besides their big department store, they owned more land than almost anybody else in all of old New York)!

Chapter Three: The Prettiest Building on the Block

Oh, you should see how pretty it is! It's made of (warm red brick with crisp stone trim, and it has a special bay window that sticks out over two whole floors, making it look as cozy and welcoming as a home). It's covered in (fancy clay decorations and beautifully carved stone all around the front door and up near the roof)!

This building is extra-special because buildings this fancy — called "Queen Anne style" — are (very rare in New York City)! It has (a tall window, a cool slanted roof, tall chimneys, and swirly decorations). The bricks are painted a (lovely rosy red color). And guess what's at the very tippy-top? (Little sunflowers made of clay)! Sunflowers on a building — how cheerful is that?

Chapter Four: What the Grown-Ups Thought — Some Nice, Some Grumpy!

Lots of people thought the building was simply lovely! One person said it (looked like it came straight out of old-fashioned London in England). Another called it (a charming little treasure made of red brick and white stone).

But there was one grumpy fellow who thought the building looked a teeny bit out of place. He grumbled that it (seemed uncomfortable sitting there in Union Square). He even said the square was (fine for showy buildings or raggedy ones, but not for a prim and proper one like this). Silly him — we think it's beautiful!

Chapter Five: The Building Full of Famous Friends

The Century magazine was a really, REALLY big deal! It printed stories by some of the most famous writers ever, like (Mark Twain, Henry James, Walt Whitman, and Herman Melville)! It even made (books by wonderful storytellers like Edith Wharton and Rudyard Kipling)! Imagine all those amazing tales being created right inside these walls!

All sorts of interesting people worked here over the years. There was even (a famous architect named George B. Post who had his office in the building)! And once upon a time, there was a company that made shirt collars! It was called Earl and Wilson, and they (made collars out of paper, shiny plastic, and stiff cloth). Their office was so modern and fancy that they even had (little electric lamps right on the desks) — which was very new and exciting back then!

Chapter Six: From Dusty and Quiet to Full of Books!

As the years went by, the building got quiet and (sat empty for a long, long time). But then — ta-da! — something magical happened in 1995. A big bookstore called (Barnes & Noble moved in and turned the giant old Century Building into a wonderful place full of books)!

A kind architect named (Judith Saltzman carefully fixed up the outside of the building), and it was so beautifully restored that it (even won an award)! Inside, they kept all the old pretty things, like the (fancy iron columns and the lovely old woodwork, so the whole place looked amazing). The very best part was (a big study room with rows of tables where you could sit and read while looking out the windows at the street below)!

Chapter Seven: A Bookstore Buzzing With Visitors

The new bookstore was so much fun that it became (a popular place for people to meet and hang out). On weekends, the store (buzzed with visitors who liked to peek at what everyone else was reading)! Famous writers even came to visit and read their books out loud to crowds — like the time (the author Margaret Atwood came to share her brand-new story)!

Chapter Eight: A Treasure Protected Forever

Because this building is so special and so beautiful, the city decided it should be kept safe forever and ever. On (October 7, 1986, it was officially named a landmark)! That means this fancy red brick building will stay safe and sound for everyone to enjoy for a very, very long time.

The End! 🌻📚
