# 44 Union Square East — The Super-Duper Story of the Coolest Building on the Corner

Tammany Hall of 1929 told as a children's story: the clubhouse with the funny name, the Liberty Cap
on the roof, the garment workers, the theatre, the film school, the ghost, and the landmark
designation of 2013.

**All 33 highlights are sourced and clickable**, and every one opens its scanned page.

**Repository path:** `brick-cakes/44-union-square-east-story/`

## How it reads

**The citation chart is on the left; the narration is on the right.**

- **Left — the citation chart.** It follows you as you scroll, and jumps to the matching entry when
  you hover a highlight. Click a number to go to that passage, or *scan* to open the page.
- **Centre — the story.** Eight sections, 33 highlights, colour-coded by source.
- **Right — the narration.** Press play and a cursor travels through the story in step with the
  reader. *Follow the reading* keeps the spoken line in view; scrolling yourself suspends it for a
  couple of seconds. Click any word outside a highlight to jump the video there.

The side columns are sticky and stack above the text below ~1360px.

Notes for the record:

- **Nothing is outstanding.** Every page the chart names was already in the series scan pool from the
  earlier 44 Union Square East build, so all 33 passages were clickable on the first build.
- **The chart arrived without page numbers**, so each source was matched to the pooled page for that
  work. One needed a judgement: Chase's *New York The Wonder City* is on file at two pages, and all
  four Chase passages — the big feelings, the chief by the river, the name known the world over, the
  earthquake relief — are filed to **p. 23**, the Tammany page. Page 198 is the Everett Building
  directory entry and has nothing to do with this building. The earthquake-relief line is the one
  worth a second look, in case it sits overleaf.
- **The scans are not marked.** In the research documents a highlight opens its page with the quoted
  lines boxed; the matcher needs five consecutive verbatim words to place a box, and these passages
  are retellings that share almost none with their sources. Marking was left off rather than have it
  refuse thirty-three times. The highlights still open the right page.
- **The narration arrived in two files and was joined here.** Part one runs 5 min 45 s, from the
  subtitle to the joke about the museum; part two is 44 s and carries *The Big Gold Star of History*.
  Both were 640×1408 H.264 with matching frame and sample rates, so they were spliced without
  re-encoding; the rail copy is 340×748.
- **The reader opens on the subtitle, not the address.** The first words spoken are *"The super-duper
  story of the coolest building on the corner"*, so the three words of the heading above it carry a
  time of zero rather than times they were never given.
- **970 of 970 spoken words are timed**, from 0 s to 388.3 s, by forced alignment against the joined
  audio. The result was checked against the recording's own pauses: seven of the eight section
  headings begin within a third of a second of a measured pause, and *The Big Gold Star of History*
  lands at 345.60 s against the file seam at 345.48 s — which confirms the two parts are in the right
  order and the timing carries across the join. *Finding the Magic Corner* has no pause before it;
  the reader runs straight on from the opening.
- **There is no score on this one.** The other documents in the series carry a music track that plays
  independently of the voice; if one is added here it drops into `assets/media/` and the page gains a
  second, separate player.
- **The trailing note about the source pages is not read aloud**, so it carries no timings and the
  cursor rests on the final applause.
