Union Square matured into a district where stone spoke a language of permanence, and granite emerged as its unifying signature—surfacing in bulkheads, columns, surrounds, and monumental pedestals alike.

At the northern gateway stands the Lincoln Building at 1 Union Square West, an early skyscraper beheld as (Romanesque Revival granite crowned with a cornice of 40 little windows with interstitial paired columns). Its fabric was (Sheathed in Indiana limestone, granite, and brick, the building is ornamented with exceptional details), and elsewhere it was (Faced with gray granite, limestone, and brick and decorated with terra cotta). A further account records (The budding, faced in limestone, granite, redbrick, and terra cotta), while its footing preserved the plainest testament—(Granite Bulkheads).

Southward, at 5 Union Square, stone persisted in both structure and ornament, its record preserving (Granite Bulkheads) and, framing its threshold, (Granite Ionic Columns).

At the southwestern corner rose the Lincoln Monument, at 15 Union Square West, where (The pedestal consists of three Dix Island granite stones, which weigh in all over forty tons, and is twenty-four feet high).

Along the western flank, 17 Union Square West presented (Granite Bulkheads) and a (Granite Surround); 21 Union Square West carried the inheritance into its foundation as (Granite Bulkheads); and 29 Union Square West named granite among its base materials—(Painted Brick, Granite)—and again at its entrance as a (Granite Surround).

The western row's most eloquent expression belongs to the Bank of the Metropolis at 31 Union Square West, whose record enumerates (Granite Bulkheads), (Polished Granite Columns), and (Granite Ionic Columns). It earned admiration for a design (enhanced by classical elements traditionally associated with American bank architecture—most notably, a bowed two-story portico with monumental polished-granite columns, lions' heads, consoles, and foliated spandrels), and equal praise as (a welcoming two-story bowed portico supported by a pair of gray granite Ionic columns facing Union Square).

The eastern edge answered with the Classical-Revival bank at 20 Union Square East, a temple wrought in stone whose record lists (Troy Granite) as base, (Troy Granite) as façade, (Granite Steps), and a (Granite Surround). It was hailed as (The bold academic classicism of this white granite bank building with its Corinthian temple front reveals the design aesthetic of Henry Bacon), confirmed where (Ihe Union Square Bank is a flat-roofed building covered in Troy white granite), and distilled in the remark that (the white granite-front structure boasts a facade of bold Corinthian columns).

Granite endured, too, in the eastern neighbors—(Granite Bulkheads) at 24 Union Square East and (Granite Bulkheads) again at 32 Union Square East. At 44 Union Square East, the Former Tammany Hall (was to be a Colonial Revival structure built of red Harvard brick with granite trim (limestone was later substituted)), while at 1 Irving Place a later building named (Brick, Granite) among its base materials and set a (Granite Surround) at its entrance. Along East 15th Street, the Swannanoa affirmed that (Its most imposing feature is the two-story base with granite pilasters and projecting portico supported by four granite columns).

The square itself endures as an open-air gallery of monuments raised upon granite. The Marquis de Lafayette ascends from a (granite pedestal); the Equestrian Statue of Washington (is of bronze, and is raised upon a granite pedestal of plain device), its form set upon a base of (14 feet high; granite pedestal), recorded as a (Bronze statue, granite base), and remembered where (The finished statue was mounted on a fourteen-foot granite pedestal designed by the noted New York architect Richard Upjohn (1802 -1878)). Mohandas Gandhi rests upon a (granite pedestal), further related where (The life-sized bronze statue rests on a granite base which together measures 14 feet in height). The Union Square Drinking Fountain incorporated a (pink granite pedestal), its ornament described where (The concept of benign nature underlies the decorative motifs of birds, fruit, flowers, and four lizards embellishing the octagonal pink granite pedestal). Abraham Lincoln stands upon a (granite pedestal) of (three Dix Island granite stones, which weigh in all over forty tons), and at the heart of the square rises the Charles F. Murphy Memorial Flagpole, anchored by a (Granite flagpole base, 36 feet in diameter).
