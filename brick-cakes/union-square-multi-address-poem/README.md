# Union Square — multi-address, poetic version

*Where the Roads Were Wed: An Elegy in Stone for the Northern Crown of Union Square* — a second
treatment of the multi-address document, written as verse. Eleven numbered movements walk the same
ground as the prose version: four on the square itself, six on the buildings around its northern
rim, and a closing instruction to look up.

Every quoted passage is highlighted and colour-coded by source. **79 of the 84 quotes are
clickable**; the remaining five are sourced but await their record cards (see below).

**Repository path:** `brick-cakes/union-square-multi-address-poem/`

## How it reads

**The citation chart is on the left**, as in the prose version. The photograph rail — empty for now —
sits on the right.

- **Left — the citation chart.** It follows you as you scroll, and jumps to the matching entry when
  you hover a highlight. Click a number to go to that quote in the text, or *scan* to open the page.
  It divides into the poem's eleven movements, so the chart reads as a walk around the square.
- **Centre — the text.** 84 highlighted quotes across eleven movements.

The side column is sticky and stacks above the text below ~1360px.

Notes for the record:

- **This version does not preserve its sources' printed errors.** Every previous document in the
  series quotes its sources exactly as printed, typos included. The verse instead normalises them —
  *founded m 1871* reads "founded in eighteen seventy-one", *Democratic Earn political machine*
  reads "Democratic political machine", *The red brick. Queen Anne-style structure* reads "the red
  brick, Queen Anne-style structure" — and spells out every numeral to keep the metre. The
  highlights therefore do not match the scans word-for-word. This is a deliberate departure for the
  poetic setting; the prose version at `union-square-multi-address/` remains the verbatim text of
  record.
- **Two record cards are still missing** — LPC *Union Square* 1893 (the Decker Building) and 1908
  (the Everett Building). Five highlights depend on them: `brick, limestone, and terra cotta`, `tile
  roof`, `Islamic star roundels`, `terra cotta, and polychrome terra cotta` and `Classical Revival`.
  All five are sourced and colour-coded; they simply have no scan to open yet. Drop the two cards
  into `assets/scans/` under those exact names and rebuild to reach 84 of 84.
- **Nine quotes were assigned by hand.** Seventy-five resolved automatically against the prose
  version and the series index; the other nine failed matching only because of the normalisation
  above. They are Chase p. 198, Lockwood p. 167, Museyon p. 208, LPC 1893, Postal & Dolkart p. 76,
  Wolfe p. 249, Watson & Gillon p. 80, LPC 1908 and Spielvogel p. 679.
- **The Decker Building carries the most weight**, at 14 quotes — more than any other building, and
  the only section to gather the building's piano-trade history and its Warhol years in one place.
- **Lockwood p. 169 is a photographic plate**, captioned "Union Square, ca. 1855". The quote assigned
  to it is not printed on the plate, so that highlight opens the photograph rather than its text. The
  page number may be off by one or two in the source chart.
- **Two different books are called *New York Then and Now*** — Watson & Gillon 1976 p. 80 and Reiss
  2019 p. 76. They are separate works with overlapping subject matter, filed under separate names;
  they sit adjacent in the source panel and look alike.
- **Section headings carry `##` prefixes in the source text.** The movements end in periods ("One.
  The Sleeping Dune."), and the builder's bare-heading detector rejects closing punctuation, so the
  prefixes are required for the chart's dividers to label correctly.

To replace or add a scan, drop it into `assets/scans/` named exactly as the citation reads and
rebuild.
