# 33 East 17th Street — second version

*The Century Building, 33 East 17th Street: An Exhaustive Architectural Treatise* — a second,
independently written treatment of William Schickel's Century Building of 1880–81, the through-block
Queen Anne commercial pile running from East 17th Street to 38–46 East 18th Street, now the Barnes &
Noble at Union Square. It covers the same building as the earlier 33 East 17th Street document but
shares none of its prose.

Nine numbered sections and four subsections, from the stylistic genealogy of the Queen Anne mode and
its Aesthetic Movement patrons, through the polychrome brick-and-bluestone wall, the entrance
ensemble, the two-storey oriel, the terra-cotta bestiary of garlands, grotesques and sunflower
finials, and the gambrel roof with its dormers, to the 1994–95 rehabilitation and the building's
contested fit within the square.

Every quoted passage is highlighted and colour-coded by source. **All 110 quotes are clickable** —
each opens its scanned source page in a panel beside the text.

**Repository path:** `brick-cakes/33-east-17th-street-2/`

## How it reads

**The citation chart is on the left**, as in 1–3 Union Square West, 31 Union Square West and 862–872
Broadway. Version 1 of this document carries it on the right. The photograph rail — empty for now —
sits on the right.

- **Left — the citation chart.** It follows you as you scroll, and jumps to the matching entry when
  you hover a highlight. Click a number to go to that quote in the text, or *scan* to open the page.
  It divides into the document's twelve labelled sections.
- **Centre — the text.** 110 highlighted quotes across nine sections and four subsections.

The side column is sticky and stacks above the text below ~1360px.

Notes for the record:

- **The LPC record card carries 32 of the 110 quotes**, supplying nearly every material and ornament
  term from section III through the Roofline. That is correct rather than a fault, but it means those
  highlights show the whole card rather than their own line on it. Spielvogel p. 291 does the same
  work for the stylistic argument, at 21.
- **Eight highlights were not listed in the supplied citation chart.** Each repeats a string the
  chart carries elsewhere, so each takes that same source rather than a guess: both bare occurrences
  of "Queen Anle pile" and the second "Sunflower Terra Cotta Finials", plus the five quotes in
  section IX, which restate passages from earlier sections.
- **One chart row covered two highlights** — the Postal & Dolkart entry written as
  `"red brick" / "to house the prestigious Century Publishing Company"` — and is applied to both.
- **The AIA scan was renamed** from *AIA Guide Edition 3* to *AIA Edition 3*, matching the supplied
  chart and the form the rest of the series uses. This resolves a title inconsistency that had run
  across three documents.
- **Stern *New York 1880* p. 440 is a plate caption, not body text.** The photograph it captions
  shows the Dominick & Haff building filling the frame; the Century Company Building is one of three
  named in the caption's left-to-right sweep, not the subject of the shot.
- **The text preserves its sources' own errors**, as printed: *Queen Anle pile*, *the braiding was
  converted*, *fames Cohen*, *die character*, *consider able original woodwork*.
- **The builder was fixed while making this document.** The citation chart took its section labels
  from a stricter heading test than the body did, so bare section titles never registered — which put
  53 rows under one subsection heading and left the first 35 unlabelled. The chart now groups into
  the correct twelve sections. Documents already published were built before the fix.

To replace or add a scan, drop it into `assets/scans/` named exactly as the citation reads and
rebuild.
